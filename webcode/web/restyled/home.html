How it works

Four small steps, every time. The first three are automatic; the last one is always yours.

01

Say what you need

Type one request in plain language, the way you'd tell a colleague. One ask per message.

02

It reads the ask

Egg works out the intent, who it's about and how risky it is. Too vague? It asks you to clarify instead of guessing.

03

It picks a capability

Routine work is handed to the right capability — a small specialist that knows how to do just that job.

04

You stay in control

Sending, paying, deleting or committing always pauses for a fresh approval, bound to the exact thing it will do.

The moving parts

Router

Reads your request and decides what kind of job it is and how risky.

Capability nodes

Small specialists — follow-up, scheduling, routing — each does one job well.

Approval gate

Anything consequential waits here until you say yes. Nothing irreversible happens on its own.

On-device model

The only place a language model is used — and it can be switched off; the rules keep working.

Scope guard

Payments, refunds, legal, hiring and deletions are refused up front, on purpose.

Activity log

Every request and outcome is recorded, so you can always see what happened and why.

What it will — and won't — do

Will handle

  • Chase a supplier or a customer
  • Draft and, on approval, send a follow-up
  • Propose a schedule change
  • Route an incoming request to the right queue

Will not do

  • Pay or purchase anything
  • Delete or destroy data
  • Refund money
  • Make legal or hiring decisions
